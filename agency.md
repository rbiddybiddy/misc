This is my summary / paraphrase of this blog post: ["Agency Is The Job Now"](https://www.drjoshcsimmons.com/writing/agency-is-the-job-now) Mostly I've managed to shorten and condense, although some sentences are copied verbatim, so sue me. (Ideas added by me that aren't in the original are in parentheses with my initials like this. -RC)

### Agency Is Primary
"High agency" is basically getting things done, by going around or through the obstacle, and doing whatever the goal requires. There's no checklist for it; it looks different every time. (But initiative, audacity, persistence, people skills and focus seem like core skills. -RC)

AI has re-priced pure technical expertise, and it won't be re-priced back. The thing whose price remained untouched is the part that was always valuable, agency. So (whereas the two previously were roughly equal in value up to a certain level in the org -RC), agency has definitively become primary. Being someone who's able to get a group pointed in a direction, able to mobilize people across a department, is worth more.

Besides AI, another trend driving this is orgs going toward a flat structure. Managers no longer have the time to challenge every initiative that crosses their desk. Put these two trends together and it means the world now belongs to people who can drive change on their own and lead technical initiatives without waiting for permission that's never coming.

### Examples
Examples of high agency according to this blogger:
- His dad paid a call on a sales prospect and brought doughnuts. The prospect wasn't in, but the doughnuts bought him a tip from the secretaries that the prospect was out giving blood. So he went to the blood drive, signed up to donate, and contrived to be seated next to the prospect, where he had a captive audience for half an hour. Lost some blood, gained a sale.
- The blogger himself, a remote worker at the time, requested something from IT and the ticket languished for months. So he flew to the office where IT was headquartered, found the right person, sat on the corner of their desk and waited while they finished and closed out the ticket. No tech skills needed, just being willing to buy a plane ticket and be somewhat ridiculous about things.
- An intern he knew needed to get a bunch of people to move on a project. So before asking anyone for anything, he built relationships through lunches, conversations about hobbies, and taking actual interest in actual humans. When the time came to mobilize them, they moved, because the intern had already done the work of becoming someone they wanted to help.

### Things That Look Like Agency But Aren't
- **Working late** - Unless you are sprinting a deadline for a few days, working outside banking hours is typically low-agency. It's an outward signal about how hard you're working, that you're broadcasting using effort that would've been better spent actually advancing something. (Also: The signal might actually be that you "need more time" i.e. that you're slow, rather than hard-working. Plus, probably nobody's there at that hour to read the signal anyway, whatever its meaning. -RC)
- **Hero culture** - Grinding alone in a vacuum is low-agency. It feels productive but actually represents a social disconnect that produces frustrated colleagues and stalled goals. The genuinely high-agency person inspires teamwork across whatever org boundaries happen to be in the way.
- **Burning up AI tokens** - You can talk to a model all day without meaningfully advancing a single goal, and it will feel & look like work the entire time.

### Some Orgs Stifle Agency
In a place where there's no tolerance for breaking norms, and everybody is afraid of looking stupid and protects himself, agency will generally be crippled and stymied. Ironically this environment will probably be the same one that loudly claims to encourage independent initiative. They might even judge you on initiative in reviews, while discouraging it year-round. If you're high-agency in a place like that, you'll keep hitting a ceiling.

### Hiring For High Agency
Stop reading resumes and ask instead for a quick 4-, 8- or 12-week plan of what the candidate would accomplish in the role. If a candidate sends one without being asked, even better. And if you're the candidate, that's the move: send them the plan.

### Nutshell
Get very clear on what your goal is and what you are trying to accomplish, every single day. That clarity is the whole discipline. Then, figure out what the doughnuts are in your situation and bring them.
